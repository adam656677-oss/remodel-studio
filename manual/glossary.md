## Glossary

Terms used in the program and in this manual. Construction and code terms are defined the way the program uses them.

**ADA 2010 Standards** — The 2010 ADA Standards for Accessible Design. The program checks door clear widths, turning space, ramp slopes, reach ranges and knee space when *ADA* is on in the Project panel (always on for IBC projects).

**AFCI** — Arc-fault circuit interrupter; a breaker that trips on arcing. Required for most 120-volt living-area circuits (IRC E3902.17).

**Aging in place** — Design choices that let someone stay in the house as they age: zero-step entries, 36" doors, lever handles, blocking for grab bars, a bedroom and full bath on the entry level. The Check page lists them as a separate review.

**Appliance** — A catalog item with a brand, a model, a published size, electrical and gas requirements and clearances. Appliances are placed like fixtures and listed on the appliance schedule and the orders list.

**Backer board** — Cement board (CBU) under tile on floors and walls. Counted on the builder's list and nested on the cut plan.

**Backsplash (splash)** — The vertical finish behind a countertop: a 4" strip of the counter material, or tile up to the wall cabinets (full height).

**Bearing wall** — A wall that carries floor, ceiling or roof load from above. The program decides bearing from joist direction, girders and the walls above; *Remove or open a wall* sizes the beam that replaces one.

**Bid** — A subcontractor's price for a trade's scope. The Bids tab makes the request, records up to three quotes per trade and compares them with the estimate.

**Binder (homeowner binder)** — The PDF hand-over book: drawings, selections, appliance models, paint colors, warranties, maintenance reminders, shut-off locations and contacts.

**Block** — A saved group of items (a bath layout, a kitchen island with stools) that can be placed again with one click. Edit › Blocks.

**Builder's list** — The quantity take-off: every material the model needs, by category, with waste, unit, quantity and price.

**Budget buckets** — The estimate grouped into spending categories (framing, cabinets, electrical…) so receipts can be compared with the plan on *Budget vs actual*.

**Cabinet nomenclature** — The industry code for a cabinet: letter for type and numbers for size. *B36* is a 36" base; *W3030* a 30" wide, 30" high wall cabinet; *SB36* a sink base; *BBC39* a blind base corner; *T2484* a tall cabinet. The catalog and the schedules use these codes.

**Change order** — A priced record of a change made after the contract was locked, with a before/after list and a signature line.

**Clash** — A pipe or duct that runs through a joist, girder or stud where it must not (notch and bore limits of IRC R502.8 and R602.6). Listed on the Check page under *Framing*.

**Clear opening** — The usable width of a door with the door open 90°, measured from the face of the door to the stop. ADA needs 32"; the IRC needs one 32" exit door.

**Climate zone** — The IECC zone (1A–7) that sets insulation and window requirements on the Energy code tab. The default is 3A.

**Color by** — A view mode that tints the plan and the 3D model by a property: remodel status, item category, vendor or brand, order status, lead time, cost, issues, bearing walls, room use or floor finish.

**Command palette** — The **Search or ask** box (**Ctrl+K**): finds commands, tools, pages, levels and catalog items, places items by name and answers questions about the project.

**Construction line** — An endless guide line that never prints; used to line things up.

**Countertop** — The work surface on base cabinets. Material, edge, thickness, overhang and splash are job settings; each counter piece gets a number on the shop drawing and a seam suggestion at inside corners.

**Crawlspace / slab / basement** — The three foundation types in the Project panel. They change the floor framing, the insulation checks and the take-off.

**Cut plan** — Sheet goods (drywall, subfloor, sheathing, backer, plywood) nested on standard sheets with a diagram, yield and a CSV cut list.

**Dashboard** — The Home tab: checks, cost, schedule, orders, field items and recent projects on one page.

**Demolish / Existing / New** — The three remodel statuses of a wall, opening or fixture. *Existing* is what is there and stays; *Demolish* is removed; *New* is built. The layer control shows any combination; the take-off counts demolition and new work.

**Design option** — A complete alternate version of the model (Option A, B, C…) inside one project, for showing a client two layouts with their own prices.

**Dimension string** — A line of dimensions along a wall: overall, corners, door and window centers. *Automatic dimensions* draws them for every exterior wall.

**Display (plan)** — The popover on the plan that turns layers on and off (rooms, wall lengths, electrical, roof lines, dimensions, notes, drafting, site), sets object snaps and picks drafting layers.

**Dormer** — A roofed projection out of a sloped roof with a window in its face.

**Drafting layer** — A named layer for drafting lines, notes and hatches that can be hidden or printed separately.

**Egress window** — A bedroom window large enough to climb out of: 5.7 sq ft clear opening (5.0 at grade), 24" high, 20" wide, sill no more than 44" above the floor (IRC R310).

**Elevation (drawing)** — A straight-on view of a wall: exterior elevations (A4 sheets) show the outside; interior elevations and kitchen elevations (A5 sheets) show cabinet walls.

**Estimate** — Materials plus labor, tax, overhead and profit, contingency, permit and extras. A planning number, printed as a proposal.

**Field pin** — A note pinned to a spot on the plan from the site: an existing condition, a punch item, a question or a photo spot, with a category, a status, a due date, photos and a voice note.

**Finish board** — A one-page sample board of the job's cabinets, counters, floors, tile, paint and hardware, with swatches.

**Fixture** — Anything placed in a room: cabinets, plumbing fixtures, appliances, furniture, built-ins. Fixtures are listed on the fixture and cabinet schedules.

**Fixture unit** — Water supply (WSFU) and drainage (DFU) units that size pipes: each fixture type has a value from the IRC tables and the totals pick the pipe sizes.

**Footprint wizard** — Draws the outside walls of a house from overall dimensions and a shape (rectangle, L, T, U).

**Framing plan** — Sheets that show joists, girders, headers, beams and rafters with sizes and spacing (S sheets).

**GFCI** — Ground-fault circuit interrupter protection, required at counters, baths, garages, outdoors and laundry (IRC E3902).

**Girder / beam** — A horizontal member that carries joists or a wall. Sized by the span tables on the Structure tab.

**Grade** — The ground level around the house; set per side in the Project panel so foundation walls, steps and ramps are the right height.

**Header** — The beam over a door or window opening. The header schedule lists each opening's size and the header the span table gives it.

**IBC 2021** — The International Building Code, used when the project is a commercial or city building. Adds occupancy groups, exit counts, corridor widths and accessible route checks.

**IFC** — Industry Foundation Classes, the open BIM file format. *File › Export › IFC* writes the model for Revit, Archicad or any IFC viewer.

**Inspector** — The right panel. It shows the properties of whatever is selected, or the Project panel when nothing is.

**Inspection prep** — Checklists for each inspection (footing, framing, rough-in, insulation, final) filled with the job's own sizes and the open check errors.

**IRC 2021** — The International Residential Code, the default code for houses. Chapters R (building), N (energy), E (electrical), P (plumbing), M (mechanical) are checked.

**Issue** — One finding of the checks: a severity (error, warning, tip), a title, the rule, the detail and the item it points to.

**Joist** — A floor or ceiling framing member. The joist dialog sizes them from span, spacing, species and load.

**KCD** — The NKBA's Kitchen and Bath Certified Designer credential; the program's kitchen drawings follow the NKBA graphics standard a KCD uses.

**Keynote** — A numbered note tag on the plan tied to a project-wide keynote list printed on its own sheet.

**Lead time** — The weeks between ordering and delivery for windows, cabinets, counters and appliances; the schedule turns it into an order-by date.

**Level** — A story of the building (basement, first floor, second floor, attic) with its own height and plan.

**Lighting scene** — Named dimmer levels for each switch group (Cooking, Dinner, Night light, Everything on), set up on Systems › Lighting and used by the Render studio.

**My standards** — Your saved defaults: cabinet line and finish, hardware, counters, estimate rates, framing, energy, code settings. New projects start from them.

**NKBA guidelines** — The NKBA Kitchen and Bath Planning Guidelines (work aisle, landing areas, clearances, seating, bath spacing). Shown as tips and warnings when NKBA is on.

**Object snap** — Snapping to endpoints, midpoints, intersections, centers, perpendiculars and nearest points while drawing.

**Option bar** — The strip above the plan that changes with the tool (wall type, door type, fixture type, 45° snap).

**Order-by date** — The latest date a long-lead item can be ordered and still arrive before its schedule phase.

**Panel schedule** — The electrical panel's circuits: space, breaker size, wire, what it feeds. Built from the devices on the plan.

**Permit package** — The checklist and the set of sheets a building department asks for, printed together.

**Photo match** — Lining up the 3D model with a real photograph of the house so the proposed work is drawn over the photo.

**Plan set** — The numbered sheets (A0 cover through S, E, P, L sheets) printed as one PDF with a title block.

**Presentation** — A widescreen PDF for client meetings: cover, before and after, renders, views, options, plans, cost, code, structure, finish board, notes.

**Project panel** — The Inspector with nothing selected: project name, snap grid, building code, ADA and NKBA switches, foundation, framing, energy, cabinets, estimate and sheet settings.

**Punch list** — The list of items to finish or fix before the job is done; field pins with the *Punch* category.

**Purchase order** — The PDF sent to a supplier for a tracked order, listing the items, sizes and the delivery date.

**Rafter / truss** — Roof framing members; the roof wizard sizes rafters or lays out trusses.

**Remodel layer** — The *Existing + new / Existing only / New only* control that filters the plan and 3D by remodel status.

**Revision** — A named snapshot of the model. A later revision can be compared with it and the changed areas clouded on the sheets with a revision number.

**Ribbon** — The toolbar on the Design page with tabs Home, Architecture, Interiors, Structure, Systems, Site, Annotate, Draw, Modify and View. It can be pinned open, collapsed to its tabs or shown as icons only.

**Riser diagram** — The plumbing drawing of drains, vents and supplies by size (P sheets).

**Schedule (document)** — A table of items of one kind with their properties: doors, windows, rooms, cabinets, hardware, lighting, fixtures, finishes, appliances.

**Schedule (job)** — The construction timeline: phases with durations from the labor hours, crew size, start date and lead times.

**Section cut** — A line through the plan that becomes a building section sheet.

**Selections** — The brands and products chosen for each category of the job (windows, roofing, flooring, counters, fixtures, appliances, paint), stamped on the builder's list and the schedules.

**Sheet** — One page of the plan set, with a number (A1.1), a name, a scale and a title block.

**Snap grid** — The invisible grid free clicks round to (6" by default).

**Span table** — The IRC tables (R502.3, R802.4, R602.7) the program uses to size joists, rafters and headers.

**Standards (My standards)** — See *My standards*.

**Tape wizard** — The *Make walls › From tape measurements* wizard that turns the tape measurements of a room (wall lengths and the offsets of doors and windows) into walls and openings.

**Takeoff** — See *Builder's list*.

**Tempered glass** — Safety glass required near doors, tubs, showers and low windows (IRC R308.4).

**Title block** — The box on every sheet with the project name, address, client, designer, sheet number and date.

**Underlay** — A scan, a plan image or a PDF placed under the plan at a known scale to trace over.

**Vendor catalog** — A manufacturer's lines, door styles, finishes, colors and SKUs with prices, imported from Excel, CSV, PDF or JSON and shared by every project.

**Wall photo** — A photo taken before drywall, pinned to a wall so the pipes and wires inside it can be found later; shown on the interior elevations and in the binder.

**Wainscot** — Tile or beadboard on the lower part of a wall to a cap height.

**Workflow** — One of the seven tabs in the tab bar (Home, Measure, Design, Check, Documents, Cost, Build), each with its own pages.

**WSFU** — Water supply fixture units; see *Fixture unit*.
